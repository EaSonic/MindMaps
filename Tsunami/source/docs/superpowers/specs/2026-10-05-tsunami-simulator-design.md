# Tsunami Simulator Design

Draft for user review • 5 October 2026

Create a separate browser entertainment simulator in `Games/Tsunami Simulator`: a detailed side view of waves hitting a protective dam, overflowing into a city, and eventually breaking the dam. The player selects Shanghai beside the Huangpu River, New York City, or a Hawaiian coastal setting, changes wave size, and watches water, buildings, people, and boats react. Success depends on readable cause and effect and realistic-looking scenery. This is a playful simulation with tuned behavior, not a scientific hazard prediction tool.

## Confirmed requirements

- Show water, a protective dam, and the city together in side view: ocean/harbor water for the coastal presets and the Huangpu River for Shanghai.
- Keep a fixed side-view camera, with **very realistic buildings and a dam that have actual three-dimensional depth**. Flat artwork alone does not satisfy these structures.
- Provide selectable **Shanghai**, **New York City**, and **Hawaii** scenes with distinct recognizable scenery. No particular Hawaiian city or island has been requested.
- Selecting any location restores a fresh undamaged scene, including when returning to a location damaged in an earlier run. Do not carry flood damage between selections.
- Let the player draw/drag to make the existing dam higher. Raising it visibly changes which waves it blocks, while repeated waves still weaken and eventually break it.
- Provide a small selectable hammer tool on the left side of the page. Use it to damage and break the dam, opening the same water barrier affected by waves.
- Provide **Start**, **Finish**, **Smaller**, and **Bigger**, following Earthquake Simulator’s controls. Smaller and Bigger change tsunami size.
- Waves above the dam spill over and flood the city. Every wave impact weakens the dam until it breaks and water rushes through. Show growing cracks before the break.
- Sufficiently large waves or moving floodwater destroy buildings.
- People run onto buildings and rooftops. Boats float and can flip in rough water. Boats visibly carry people; passengers can fall overboard and then swim in the waves.
- Boats and people look realistic and recognizable. People wear clothing in varied colors.
- Rescue boats arrive at the end. Some rescue boats can flip over in the water.
- Include only a small number of large international travel/transport ships. Some can tilt and progressively sink with a dramatic Titanic-like motion as a visual reference.
- Include city cars: some float and drift, while others remain submerged underwater.
- People can drown and remain visible in very deep water, represented non-graphically. The side view lets the player see people, cars, and other submerged entities beneath the water surface.
- Trees eventually topple and streetlights eventually fall under flood forces.
- Rubbish bins float up and spill rubbish that floats **just next to the bins**. Keep the rubbish localized rather than spreading it throughout the scene.
- Provide a side palette with distinct **Person**, **Ordinary Boat**, **Rescue Boat**, and **Rescuer** choices, including different boat variants. Select a choice, then tap/click the scene to place it. New entities must be small and correctly proportioned relative to the city and dam.
- Buildings, boats, people, moving water, and splashes must look detailed and believable; repeated cubes do not meet the visual requirement.

Earthquake Simulator and Driving remain separate projects. The reference was inspected read-only: `src/components/ControlBar.tsx`, `src/hooks/useSimulation.ts`, `src/rendering/CanvasRenderer.ts`, the style files, design document, fidelity ledger, and concept artwork. Its green Start, red Finish, blue size controls, navy frame, five strength levels, top selection bar, and scene-rebuild behavior inform this design. Use location selection in place of landmark selection. Finish freezing the current result is a proposed convention from the reference, not a confirmed tsunami requirement.

## Proposed assumptions

Use three realistically rendered location presets, each with one fictional protective dam and a fixed camera. Start launches recurring waves, allowing repeated impacts to reveal progressive damage, followed by an automatic rescue phase. Use five size levels, initially level 2. Proposed initial selection is Shanghai; a location change resets size to level 2, matching the reference's selection convention. No scores, freeform city construction, campaign, country travel system, or multiplayer.

**Proposed rendering scope beyond the confirmed structures:** use detailed 3D boats, ships, cars, street props, and modestly rigged people for coherent materials, lighting, and motion; render water as a shaded deforming mesh with foam/spray effects. Distant skyline plates may support atmosphere, but foreground buildings and the dam must remain real volumetric geometry. Full volumetric fluid simulation and freely navigable 3D physics are not required by the confirmed side-view experience. The degree of 3D detail for these other objects is a proposed technical choice.

**Finish without changing location remains a proposed assumption:** immediately stop and freeze the current result for inspection, matching Earthquake Simulator. Start then rebuilds that same location before running again. The user has confirmed a clean reset when selecting another location, but has not directly confirmed whether Finish alone should freeze or rebuild. This design keeps the two actions distinct for review; all Finish-freeze behavior and checks below depend on this assumption.

**Proposed interpretation of “at the end”:** after the dam breaches, allow two additional waves to arrive at the dam position, stop emitting waves, then bring in rescue boats once the last emitted wave reaches that position. Arrival is counted even when the barrier is already broken. Run rescue for about 20 seconds before automatically freezing the result. This gives every completed run a rescue phase while preserving immediate Finish for an early stop. Early Finish does not launch rescue. Rescue timing is a design choice for review, not a confirmed user instruction.

**Building lights are an unconfirmed proposal:** lit windows briefly flicker after serious flooding and then go dark. If accepted, use a restrained fade and at most two soft flickers, with no flicker under reduced motion. Core flooding and destruction do not depend on this option.

Further proposed choices are rooftop routes through visible stairs/fire escapes and immediate freezing on Finish. Non-graphic drowning is confirmed; its exposure thresholds are proposed gameplay tuning. All numeric values below are gameplay starting points, not physical measurements.

## Approach and tradeoffs

**Recommended: fixed side-view 3D rendering with a focused water/damage simulation.** React and TypeScript own controls; a dedicated simulation owns water, damage, and actors. Use a WebGL renderer, proposed Three.js, with volumetric building and dam meshes, credible material maps, environmental light, contact shadows, and real section depth. A coarse water grid supplies continuous flood levels and currents to a deforming water mesh; foam, spray, reflections, and an underwater pass provide detail. The camera stays side-on with a slight fixed oblique angle that reveals facade returns, roofs, and dam thickness, without orbit controls. Retain the reference's interaction conventions while satisfying the explicit 3D structure requirement.

An animation-only approach could look polished quickly, but fixed clips would poorly support changing wave size, cumulative damage, and interrupted runs. A full volumetric fluid solver and unrestricted 3D world add substantial complexity and tuning risk for this fixed side view. Flat layered artwork would not meet the confirmed 3D buildings/dam requirement. The recommended approach concentrates effort on believable geometry, materials, light, and responsive water/objects. GPU performance, underwater compositing, and visual realism require rendered validation.

## Scene and visual quality

The water occupies the left third, a thick concrete dam near the center divides water and land, and the city fills the right half. A visible riverbed/seabed and slightly rising city terrain explain where water moves. Keep the crest, dam face, street, and reachable roofs in frame. Use a fixed, slightly oblique orthographic side camera that shows physical structure depth while retaining a readable horizontal simulation plane. Avoid large perspective distortion or camera movement that hides the city behind the dam. Shanghai uses river water in this composition; New York and Hawaii use harbor/coastal water. The wave source and dam are fictional entertainment layouts rather than claims about actual local defenses or tsunami behavior.

Keep three labeled location buttons across the top, styled like the reference's landmark choices. Each preset changes its skyline, foreground architecture, terrain dressing, waterfront, vegetation, and art palette while sharing the water/damage/actor mechanics. Scenery should identify a location before the player reads its label; changing only a background color is insufficient.

Stop the old loop, invalidate its queued spawns and rescue events, dispose of mutable world state, and create the target preset from its pristine definition. Restore intact buildings/dam/tree/light attachments, upright props, clean baseline water with dry city streets, afloat intact ships/boats, living actors at their initial positions, zero damage/waterlogging/submersion timers, and no debris or spilled rubbish. Clear user additions, palette selection, active drawing strokes, and dam-height edits; use that preset's original dam height. Proposed size resets to level 2. No damaged world is stored for later restoration.

Revisiting Shanghai after damaging Shanghai and playing New York must restore Shanghai's original arrangement and artwork, not its old flooded snapshot. A fixed baseline seed per preset makes that clean initial arrangement repeatable. Cache only immutable art/definitions. During artwork loading, show the selected location's loading state and disable Start/placement; ignore stale loads or commands from an older selection. Rapid switching must finish on the last selected clean preset, with one world and one loop.

The city contains roughly six distinct foreground buildings per preset: low shops, brick apartments with fire escapes, glass offices, and taller roof refuges. Model coherent proportions, facade depth, recessed windows and frames, balconies, doors, roof equipment, ledges, and varied silhouettes. Use concrete/brick/stone/glass materials with texture variation, roughness, surface relief, weathering, and sensible reflected light. The dam has substantial wall thickness, a top ledge, base, joints, wet concrete, and visible side faces. Light direction, cast/contact shadows, and material response must agree across structures, actors, and water; repeated extruded boxes with flat windows are insufficient.

Destructible buildings are volumetric structural sections with matching simplified collision shapes. Broken sections retain detailed meshes and expose plausible interior/slab surfaces instead of becoming cubes or flat image slices. Dam cracks combine surface detail with progressively separated geometry; breach removes a real wall section with thickness and broken edges. Distant scenery may be lighter weight but cannot substitute for the interactive 3D foreground.

Use two or three recognizably different ordinary boats, with shaped hulls, decks, cabins or masts, railings, and hull reflections, plus distinct rescue boat variants. Their decks visibly carry passengers. People have realistic head/body proportions, recognizable clothing in varied colors, arms and legs, and walk/run/climb/swim poses. Foreground people and passengers remain readable through contrast and animation while retaining small, consistent world proportions. Detail must remain visible while objects move, not only in an intact backdrop.

Add one large passenger transport ship offshore by default, with no crowded fleet. Its long shaped hull, multiple decks, cabin windows, bridge, railings, and funnel should suggest international travel. A transport-ship asset variant may use a cargo silhouette, but no country selection or travel gameplay is added. Keep the ship visibly larger than ordinary/rescue boats and physically consistent with the same scene scale; proposed length is 24–30 scene units compared with 4–10 for small boats. Leave enough ocean area and depth to show its tilt and descent without obscuring the dam or city. Large-ship count is limited to two; the initial scene uses one, and large ships are scene content rather than a new palette category.

Water uses a continuous shaded body and moving curved crest, translucent shallows, darker depth, reflected light, shoreline wetting, and streaks aligned with current. White foam forms at wave crests, dam impacts, overflow edges, and breach jets. Spray and droplets fall back into the water; small eddies and wakes appear around boats and obstacles. Water must visibly pass in front of submerged portions of buildings and boats without covering dry roofs or entire objects indiscriminately. Splashes decorate actual contacts; they do not substitute for flood movement.

Use a deliberate underwater cutaway within the same fixed 3D side view: submerged portions receive translucent blue-green tint, restrained refraction, softer lighting, and depth shading while retaining recognizable silhouettes and clothing/car colors. Use the sampled waterline and depth to shade dry and submerged mesh portions differently. Keep people, cars, fallen poles/trees, boat hulls, and debris visible even in deep water; cap obscuring tint and foam rather than making deep areas opaque. Water transparency must not reveal entities through solid buildings or dam sections. Drowned people remain visible below the surface in a still, fully clothed pose without blood, injury detail, or graphic effects. This readability is a deliberate choice within realistic materials and lighting.

Place varied compact cars, a sedan, and a van along the street, with wheels, windows, mirrors, body contours, and distinct colors. Trees have textured trunks, branches, and irregular foliage; streetlights have recognizable poles, lamp heads, and bases. Rubbish bins have detailed lids, handles, wheels where appropriate, and distinct materials. Match these props to the same world scale as people and architecture. All must retain recognizable detail when tilted, floating, or underwater.

Match the reference's navy control frame and large labeled buttons. Place the location selector above the scene and a **Tsunami Size** indicator between Finish and Smaller on desktop. Add a narrow labeled palette alongside the scene, with thumbnails large enough to distinguish boat choices, and a compact tool strip on the left containing Pencil, Eraser, and a small recognizable hammer icon labeled **Hammer**. The icon is small but its touch target is at least 44 pixels. On narrow screens, keep the location choices accessible in a scrollable top row, retain the tools on the left, use a compact scrollable palette beside the scene, place the size indicator above the four buttons, and fit the scene without stretching artwork. Keep essential action visible; reduce decorative detail first. Palette thumbnails may be large for selection; they do not set the scale of placed entities.

## Palette and scene placement

The interaction is **select, then place**: clicking/tapping a palette item selects its entity or boat variant and highlights it; the next valid click/tap in the map creates one instance there. Selecting the item alone never spawns an entity at an arbitrary location. Keep the selection active for repeated placements; selecting the active item again or pressing Escape cancels it. A pointer preview shows placement size and validity; touch users receive a brief placement marker after tapping. Plain scene taps without a selection add nothing.

| Palette choice | Appearance and proposed placement behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Person | Civilian with varied clothing colors. Place on a reachable street/roof, an available boat deck position, or in water as a swimmer. Follow normal rooftop evacuation during an active wave phase. |
| Ordinary Boat | Separate skiff, fishing boat, and cabin motorboat thumbnails. Place the chosen variant on the local water surface, with visible passengers and normal flotation/capsizing behavior. |
| Rescue Boat | Separate rescue inflatable and rescue launch thumbnails with recognizable markings and crews. Place on water; immediately use rescue steering/pickup behavior during a run. Automatic end-of-sequence arrivals still occur. |
| Rescuer | A separately selectable person with recognizable rescue clothing/equipment and varied uniform accents. Place on street/roof, an available deck, or water. Use normal physical movement and swimming; a rescuer at a reachable boat/shore boarding point assists nearby pickup. This assistance is a proposed behavior. |

Placement is proposed to be available in Ready and both running phases. Ready additions remain present when Start begins. In Finished, disable placement to preserve the frozen result and display “Start a new run to place more.” Restart recreates the selected scene and clears all added entities, pending placement commands, and rescue arrivals. Preserve the selected wave size and committed dam height for a repeat test, but clear palette selection. Reselecting a location instead returns to its original dam height and Ready. These same-location restart choices are proposed for review.

Project pointer coordinates through the actual fixed camera: raycast eligible foreground surfaces and the water/interaction plane, then resolve to simulation world coordinates. Distant scenery is not a placement target. Boats require water deep enough for their hull and free space; snap their waterline to the surface at the selected horizontal position. People snap only to a nearby reachable ground/roof surface or free deck slot; taps in water create swimmers. Reject taps in the sky, solid walls, dry boat positions, or occupied/full deck slots with a brief reason instead of spawning inside an obstacle. Route each accepted placement through one command so a single touch cannot create both touch and mouse duplicates. New boats start afloat with stable initial velocity; new swimmers start at the sampled surface.

Use one world scale for default and placed entities: proposed human height 1.7 scene units, typical floor height 3.2, dam height above calm sea 18, ordinary boat length 4–7, and rescue boat length 8–10. These are artwork proportions, not real measurements. Passengers and separately placed people use the same height; new boats use the same dimensions as default boats of their variant. Do not enlarge map sprites to match palette thumbnails or to compensate for a smaller viewport. Maintain readability through color, poses, and silhouette. Bound additions at 80 people including crew/passengers and 16 boats, reserving two boat slots and their crew for automatic rescue arrivals; report a reached limit without replacing existing entities. Limits are proposed performance choices.

## Drawing a taller dam

Add a labeled **Pencil** drawing tool beside the entity palette, separated from its four entity categories. Selecting it deselects entity placement and highlights the existing 3D crest. Drag upward from the highlighted crest: a thick, solid opaque concrete mesh preview follows the pointer's projected world height and raises the wall to a level top while retaining its thickness. Release commits the height. The stroke changes the dam rather than creating another wall or painting arbitrary structures. An enlarged screen-space hit area anchored to the crest makes it easy to grab by touch; show a short “Drag the dam top upward” hint when selected.

Keep exactly one input mode active: none, place an entity, pencil/raise the dam, erase added dam height, or hammer the dam. Choosing any tool/category cancels the other mode and any uncommitted preview. Drawing never places an entity or applies a hammer hit. Escape cancels an unfinished stroke and restores its previously committed height; selecting a location cancels both the stroke and its preview. Downward movement cannot lower the wall. Clamp the preview from the current crest to a proposed maximum of 2.5 times the preset's original above-water dam height, within the visible scene. A gesture without a valid upward change commits nothing. Keyboard users focus the crest and use Up/Down to adjust an upward-extension preview, Enter to commit, and Escape to cancel.

**Proposed editing policy:** allow drawing only in Ready. Disable it during waves, rescue, and Finished, so the user sets the wall before the run. Drawing during an active wave is not confirmed. Start cancels an unfinished preview and uses the last committed height. A same-location Start after Finish restores the intact dam at its committed edited height for another test; choosing any location resets its height to the original preset value. Both replay retention and the height limit are proposed choices.

**Eraser:** select the small Eraser beside Pencil/Hammer, then drag downward from the drawn extension to remove added height. Its solid preview and committed height update the real 3D mesh and water-blocking crest. Clamp at the original preset crest: erase only player additions, never the original dam. Ready-only editing is the chosen default; drawing, erasing, hammering, and placement are mutually exclusive. A single click on the extension removes a small height step; an upward/no-op gesture changes nothing. Escape cancels the preview.

The rendered 3D crest, dam collision/flow boundary, and damage geometry use the same committed height. Incoming wave levels remain tied to the preset's **original** dam height, not its edited height: raising the wall must not secretly increase the waves too. A crest that overtops the original wall but stays below the new top now splashes against the wall without flooding the city. Waves above the new top still spill over. Each wave still damages the dam, and it ultimately breaches at zero integrity; raising it does not heal damage, add unlimited integrity, or make it indestructible. Extend the mesh and world-scale concrete texture without stretching materials, update shadows/crack paths, and open the breached geometry using the edited wall shape.

## Hammering the dam

Select the **Hammer** on the left, then click/tap the visible intact dam to apply one hit. Highlight the valid dam target and show a short impact mark, a small chip burst, and an advancing crack at contact. Keep Hammer selected for repeated taps; tapping elsewhere does nothing and shows a brief “Tap the dam” hint. A drag/hold is not repeated hammering. Use one pointer action per hit to avoid duplicate touch/mouse damage. Keyboard users can select Hammer, focus the dam target, and press Enter for one hit.

**Proposed hammer tuning:** each accepted hit removes 20 from the dam's current integrity, clamped at zero, so an untouched 100-integrity dam takes five hits. Wave damage can reduce the remaining hit count. Hit strength/count are implementation choices, not confirmed values. Record hammer hits as commands applied before the next water substep. Waves and hammering modify one shared integrity value, crack state, and breach transition; they must not maintain separate decorative and physical damage. At zero, detach the same textured section and open the water boundary immediately. The next transport substep drives sea/river water through the breach into the city. The breach also triggers the normal remaining-wave/rescue sequence regardless of whether waves or hammering caused it. Hitting detached rubble or an already broken dam is a no-op.

**Proposed availability:** Hammer works during an active wave phase. In Ready show “Start before hammering the dam”; in Finished keep it disabled to preserve the assumed frozen result. The dam is normally already breached during rescue, so there is no intact target then. Availability before Start or after Finish is unconfirmed. This policy makes hammer-caused flooding immediately observable within the running simulation, while Ready remains the setup state for drawing/placement. Finish cancels pending hammer commands and freezes the result. Start-after-Finish rebuilds undamaged integrity at the committed height; choosing a location clears hammer mode, damage, impacts, and queued hits along with all other state.

| Ready | Intact dam and city, dry streets, people at starting positions, boats afloat. Size changes are available. |
| Start | Cancel an uncommitted drawing preview and enter running at the committed dam height; send the first crest toward the dam, arriving after about six seconds, then repeat about every seven seconds. People immediately begin rooftop evacuation. Start is disabled while running. |

Repeated actions never create duplicate loops, waves, or placement events. Size changes in the finished state select the next run’s size without altering the frozen result. Resizing the window rescales the view; it does not rebuild the world or change entity proportions.

## Water and damage behavior

Represent water depth and horizontal flow over fixed terrain columns. Waves are bounded source pulses entering from the ocean side. The intact dam blocks transfer below its crest; excess water crosses the crest into land cells, making a visible falling sheet and downstream splash. Transfer removes water from the sea side and adds it to the land side. After breach, the boundary opens down to the broken section’s base and produces a much stronger current. Building columns resist flow but retain a tuned permeable street channel, representing water passing beside buildings in this side view. Floodwater pools in low terrain and can recede between pulses. The ocean inlet and far-land outlet are explicit boundaries.

Dam integrity starts at 100. Deduct damage once per arriving wave, identified by wave ID, rather than once per rendered frame. Cracks darken and branch as integrity falls; small chips precede a large opening. At zero, detach a textured dam section and open the water boundary in the same simulation step. Remaining rubble can obstruct flow, but cannot restore an intact barrier.

| Size | Incoming crest height relative to original preset dam height above calm water | Integrity lost per impact | Intended early result with original dam |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 0.55 | 6 | Gentle boat motion; dam initially holds. |
| 2 | 0.85 | 10 | Stronger impacts; visible cracking before flooding. |
| 3 | 1.20 | 16 | Clear overtopping while the dam still stands. |
| 4 | 1.70 | 24 | Large overflow, strong currents, building damage. |
| 5 | 2.30 | 36 | Severe overflow, plausible capsizing, rapid breach and collapse. |

These choices guarantee eventual breach even under repeated small waves, while higher levels reach it sooner. Damage persists after reducing size. Overtopping and breach are separate observable events.

Buildings accumulate stress from local water depth and current force, with different resistance by construction. Low-force contact wets facades and moves small debris. Strong currents crack supports and detach lower sections; unsupported upper sections tilt, fall, or break apart. Flood depth alone does not make every building instantly disappear. Major debris interacts with water and remaining structures using simple bounded collision shapes; small fragments are decorative and capped.

Boats sample water beneath bow and stern for lift, pitch, and drag. Calm water supports them at their waterline. Rough crests and impact currents produce torque; beyond a tuned stability threshold a boat capsizes and remains visibly overturned. Do not flip boats through unrelated random timers.

Large transport ships use several buoyancy samples along the hull and separate waterlogging values for bow, middle, and stern. Mild waves produce slow rolling without instant sinking. Severe repeated loading can damage the hull and increase waterlogging unevenly. As buoyancy falls, one end sits progressively lower, the ship develops a list, the bow can descend while the stern rises prominently, and the hull gradually passes below the surface. Tune a severe run to show this dramatic Titanic-like sinking motion, retaining the original fictional ship, scenery, and intact hull; do not recreate a historical wreck or require a ship breakup. Uneven buoyancy drives the tilt rather than an unrelated timer or an instant disappearance. Sunk ships remain visible through the underwater tint and can settle on the seabed. People visible aboard move with the deck and can fall into swimming/submerged states as it tilts or goes underwater. Their scale, drowning rules, and rescue interactions match other people.

Passengers initially stand or sit at distinct deck positions and follow their boat's translation and rotation. Sharp pitch, acceleration, or capsizing can release them over the rail into the water. Show a short fall and contact splash, then a swimming state with visible arm strokes and the head normally near the surface. Swimmers rise and fall with waves and drift with the current; strokes add modest movement toward an accessible shore or refuge, rather than allowing them to outrun a large surge. Strong local downward motion, debris contact, or exhaustion can carry them below the surface. Shore exit and a return to rooftop routes are proposed recovery behavior. Swimmers remain visible between passing foam and splashes; they must not vanish immediately after falling overboard.

People follow explicit street-to-stairs-to-roof paths, with varied starts and speeds. Roof positions attach to their building sections. If a route becomes flooded or a roof becomes unsafe, redirect toward another reachable higher refuge. People displaced into water use the same swimming state as fallen passengers. Those swept beyond the view leave the scene without graphic injury; no walking through broken walls, hovering over fallen roofs, or implied guaranteed rescue.

In deep water, accumulate submerged-head exposure only while the head is actually below the sampled surface. Proposed tuning: water deeper than three human heights and about eight continuous seconds of head submersion can transition a person from swimming/submerged to drowned; resurfacing clears that continuous timer. Rough-water exhaustion weakens upward swimming, making this condition reachable without forcing every swimmer to drown. A drowned person stops active strokes, sinks slowly, and settles or drifts underwater as an inert visible figure. Keep that entity in the scene until reset or it moves beyond the view; never remove an in-view person merely because they drowned. This applies to civilians, boat passengers, and rescuers. Pickup targets living swimmers; it does not silently turn a drowned person back into a swimmer. Thresholds and motion are entertainment behavior, not survival guidance.

## Flooded city objects

Cars respond to local depth, current, buoyancy, and simplified collisions. Give different cars different mass and waterlogging rates: a light compact can lift off the street and drift near the surface, while a heavier or waterlogged vehicle sinks and remains visibly submerged on the road or bed. Floating cars may tilt or later sink as waterlogging progresses; submerged cars can still slide under strong flow. Tune the default severe run to show both floating and underwater cars together rather than making all vehicles follow one identical animation. Cars are existing scene props; the confirmed four palette categories remain unchanged.

Trees and streetlights accumulate damage from flood drag and bending force. Trees first lean, then lose their root attachment and topple; poles bend or loosen before their base fails and they fall. After failure, their recognizable shapes remain collision-aware props that can settle or drift according to their buoyancy. Sustained flooding eventually topples vulnerable trees and drops streetlights; stronger flow does it sooner. Falling streetlights are confirmed independently of the optional building-window flicker.

When water reaches a bin, buoyancy lifts it and the current can tip it or open its lid. Release a few recognizable wrappers, bottles, or small bags from the bin opening, then float them alongside that specific bin. **Rubbish must stay just next to its source bin**, including as the bin drifts. Use a small bounded patch, proposed within roughly two bin widths, with gentle relative motion and the bin's shared current drift. Do not emit rubbish across the whole map or leave a spreading trail behind a moving bin. Cap each bin at eight visible rubbish pieces; each piece keeps its source-bin ID, and the local cluster resets with the bin. These localization and count choices keep the user's corrected placement explicit.

## Rescue phase

At the end of the wave sequence, two recognizable rescue boats arrive from offshore with rescue crews, distinct hull markings, railings, and visible occupants. They navigate toward the breach and reachable swimmers using modest steering thrust while continuing to respond to local waves and currents. A slowed boat within boarding distance collects a swimmer onto a free deck position. Roof occupants can board only when the boat is close enough to a surviving accessible ledge; they cannot teleport across dry streets. Rescue pickup is proposed behavior that gives the arriving boats a clear purpose.

Rescue boats use the same buoyancy, drag, and torque model as other boats, with higher stability thresholds. Strong residual currents, debris contact, or rough water can still capsize some of them. Crew and rescued passengers then fall and swim using the existing actor states; rescue boats must not have immunity or flip on arbitrary timers. Tune a severe scenario to visibly demonstrate at least one rescue-boat capsize, while milder conditions permit successful pickup. End-of-sequence arrivals remain automatic in addition to rescue boats placed through the palette, with the four primary simulation controls unchanged. The rescue phase lasts about 20 seconds, then preserves the final scene for inspection; it does not imply every person was rescued.

## Architecture and stability

Keep the new app self-contained; do not import runtime code or assets by reaching into another game’s directory. Follow the reference’s React/TypeScript/Vite convention when implementation is approved, with no dependency work at this stage.

| Unit | Responsibility and interface |

Each fixed tick applies placement and hammer commands, commits resulting barrier changes, and updates wave sources, then repeats water transport and damage evaluation over bounded substeps. A breach immediately changes the obstacle boundary for the next transport substep; the published snapshot includes matching cracks, openings, and debris. Props and actors advance after water and damage. Use a 60 Hz fixed simulation step, a capped catch-up budget, and interpolated rendering. Limit transfer to available water, keep dry cells stable, and reject non-finite values. Suspend elapsed-time accumulation in a hidden tab so returning does not cause an enormous jump. Seeded scenario runs make failures reproducible.

Cache textures, cap decorative particles and debris, and limit pixel density. Reduce foam, reflections, and background detail under load while preserving wave timing, damage, and actor behavior. Reduced motion removes decorative shake and flicker and reduces spray, retaining essential flow and evacuation. Keyboard focus and touch targets follow the reference. Palette items have names and selected states; after keyboard selection, a focused scene placement marker moves with arrow keys and Enter places the entity. Announce placement acceptance/rejection and major state changes, overtopping, and breach through restrained text updates rather than per-frame narration.

Missing essential model/material assets produces a clear retry state with Start disabled; a generic block scene is not an acceptable final fallback. Unavailable WebGL or a lost GPU context produces a readable rendering error with a reload action. Dispose of GPU geometry/material/texture resources when replacing a scene. Reset/unmount cancels the sole animation loop and clears scheduled events and particles. If simulation values become invalid, enter finished at the last valid snapshot with a restart message and Start available.
